What breaks when lemmas are deleted?

Delete main-library statements one at a time and track the largest connected component (citations treated as undirected). Single deletions never matter here: the worst single node or edge cuts off 3 theorems. The question is how many deletions it takes, and in what order.

Sandbox: change the assumptions

Switch principles off and on, or add your own, and both maps below are recomputed in your browser: which logics exist, how they relate, and a certificate for every step (a countermodel, or a proof via the exact decision procedures). Propositional formulas go on the first map (on top of minimal logic); anything with □ or ◇ goes on the modal map (on top of K). Click a principle's name to find its logic on the map.

Map of logics

Each box is a logic: minimal logic plus the principles named in it. A line means the upper logic proves everything the lower one assumes, and is certified strictly stronger by a countermodel. Logics that prove each other's principles are merged into one box. The bar shows how much of the generated classical library the logic proves (range when only bounds are known). Principles

The weakest of minimal ⊂ intuitionistic ⊂ classical that proves each principle (decided exactly). If false, at least: among small worlds (truth tables with up to 4 values) where minimal logic holds but the principle is false, the one that breaks the fewest library theorems, and how many it still breaks. An upper bound on the true minimum, since only small worlds are searched.

Map of modal logics

The same method with □ ("necessarily"). Every logic is K plus the principles named in it: D, T, B, 4, 5 (the "modal cube"), .2 and Löb's axiom. Countermodels are Kripke frames with up to 4 worlds; proofs come from an exact K decision procedure applied to instances of the axioms. The 32 combinations of D, T, B, 4, 5 collapse into exactly 15 logics, as the textbooks say. Click a box or a line.
